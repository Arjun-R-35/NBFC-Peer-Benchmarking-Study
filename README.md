# NBFC Peer Benchmarking Study: Q4 FY26 vs Q1 FY27

An Excel-based comparison of five Indian NBFCs on asset quality, capital adequacy, growth, profitability and funding cost, built to practise credit analysis and financial statement analysis.

## Companies covered
Bajaj Finance, Cholamandalam Investment and Finance, Shriram Finance, Muthoot Finance, Five-Star Business Finance

## Metrics
GNPA %, NNPA %, CRAR %, AUM growth % (YoY), ROA %, Cost of funds %

## What's in the workbook
| Sheet | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Summary | Comparison table, charts, key findings, conclusion, and Better/Worse overview of the quarter-on-quarter change |
| QoQ_Change | Change (in percentage points) from Q4 FY26 to Q1 FY27 for each metric, with direction |
| Clean_data | Standardised figures with data-quality notes |
| Raw_data | Source figures with document and page references, and whether each is Reported or Computed |

## Method
- Figures were taken from investor presentations, unaudited financial results, press releases and one earnings-call disclosure.
- Where a metric was not reported, it was computed from disclosed inputs (for example, ROA = annualised PAT ÷ average total assets) and marked **Computed**.
- Change is shown in percentage points. Direction is **Better** or **Worse** depending on whether a higher or lower value is favourable.

## Key observations
- Bajaj Finance has the lowest stressed assets (GNPA 0.96%) and improved on most metrics.
- Muthoot Finance's asset quality improved, but its AUM growth and ROA both fell.
- Five-Star Business Finance has the largest capital buffer (CRAR 51.25%) but slower growth.
- Shriram Finance's CRAR rose sharply from 20.40% to 34.17%.

## Limitations
- Definitions differ across companies (Stage 3 vs GNPA/NNPA, cost of funds basis, standalone vs consolidated).
- Two quarters show direction of change, not a long-term trend.
- Bajaj Finance's Q4 FY26 figures are after one-time actions; pre-adjustment values are noted in Raw_data.
- The study describes direction of change and does not rank the companies overall.

## Tools
